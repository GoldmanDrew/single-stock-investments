## What this business is

American Express Company (NYSE: AXP) operates a closed-loop payments network that connects affluent card members, merchants, and issuing partners worldwide. **[Fact]** FY2025 revenues net of interest expense were **$72.2 billion**, net income **$10.83 billion**, and diluted earnings per share **$15.38** (`investor-documents/sec-edgar/10-K_20260206_rpt20251231_acc0000004962_26_000080.htm`). **[Fact]** The company earns spread and fee income on card lending, merchant discount revenue, and network services, funded partly by customer deposits and wholesale funding. **[Inference]** Owner economics are normalized cash generation after funding the card receivable book and technology spend, not a simple industrial free cash flow line.

---

## Why the market might be wrong

The market may be pricing American Express as a mature compounder whose premium card franchise already captures most of its reinvestment runway. **[Inference]** Q2 2026 operating revenue of **$14.8 billion** (net interest income **$4.6 billion** plus non-interest income **$10.2 billion**) shows the fee-heavy model still growing versus prior year (`investor-documents/sec-edgar/10-Q_20260724_rpt20260630_acc0000004962_26_000322.htm`). **[Inference]** Return is earnings power and franchise durability at price, not a dated catalyst. Upside requires sustained high-return reinvestment in card member acquisition and merchant acceptance; downside is credit normalization and funding-cost pressure on a levered balance sheet.

---

## Executive summary

At **$351.93** (Yahoo close 2026-07-20), our base case annual return is **2.3%** per year over seven years on proof-backed economic value of **$412.52** per share. American Express is a global payments compounder with a narrow moat from its closed-loop network and premium brand. FY2025 normalized owner earnings were **$16.0 billion** (cash from operations **$18.4 billion** minus capital spending **$2.4 billion**). The stock trades above our low-case value of **$228.65** per share but below the high case of **$786.43**. Dhando is **partial**: credit losses are bounded by affluent card member mix, but funding and valuation leave limited margin of safety at today's price. Proposed stance: **pass** (below our **~15%** annual return hurdle).

## Risks & inversion

**Primary risk:** Credit losses and funding costs rise together in a recession, compressing returns on the card receivable book while rewards spending stays elevated to retain card members.

- Consumer credit deterioration could force reserve builds beyond current provision levels (`10-K_20260206`, credit quality discussion).
- Funding spread compression: wholesale and deposit costs may outrun card lending yields if rates fall unevenly (`10-Q_20260724`, net interest income).
- Competitive rewards pressure from bank issuers on Visa and Mastercard rails could raise customer acquisition cost (`10-K`, competition section).
- Valuation risk: at **~$352** per share, the market already capitalizes durable franchise economics, leaving low annual return at base proof value.

| Risk | Mechanism | Falsifier |
