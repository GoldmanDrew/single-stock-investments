## What this business is

Centene Corporation (NYSE: CNC) is the largest U.S. Medicaid and Health Insurance Marketplace insurer and a major Medicare Advantage and prescription drug plan administrator. **[Fact]** The company collects premiums and capitated payments from state Medicaid agencies, the federal Medicare program, and individual Marketplace customers, then pays medical claims and earns a spread on medical management, pharmacy, and administrative services. **[Fact]** FY2025 consolidated revenue was **$194.8 billion**; normalized owner earnings (cash from operations minus capital spending) were **$4.3 billion** on **$5.1 billion** of operating cash flow. **[Fact]** Reportable segments are Medicaid, Medicare, Commercial (Marketplace and employer), and Other. **[Inference]** The equity is a scaled managed-care compounder on government-program cash flows, but earnings quality is dominated by medical-cost trend, Medicaid redeterminations, and periodic goodwill charges rather than hidden assets.

---

## Why the market might be wrong

The market may be treating the **FY2025 net loss of $6.7 billion** (driven by a **$6.7 billion** goodwill impairment on Magellan Health) as a permanent cash-flow impairment, while underweighting **normalized owner earnings near $4.3 billion**, **$24.2 billion** of cash at mid-2026, and a share price **36% below** our base intrinsic value per share. **[Inference]** Return is not a dated catalyst story; it is **earnings power at a discount** if medical-cost trend stabilizes after Medicaid redeterminations and Marketplace mix shifts. **[Opinion]** The inefficiency is headline risk on reported losses and Medicaid membership volatility, not an undisclosed asset mark.

## Executive summary

At **$64.34** (Yahoo CNC close 2026-08-31), our base case annual return is **6.6% per year** over seven years on normalized owner earnings of **$4.3 billion** (FY2025 cash from operations of **$5.1 billion** minus capital spending of **$767 million**). Centene is the nation's largest Medicaid and Marketplace insurer, with Medicare Advantage weighted toward dual-eligible special-needs populations. FY2025 revenue reached **$194.8 billion**, but reported net income was a **$6.7 billion** loss after goodwill and intangible impairments tied to Magellan Health; first-half 2026 net income was **$2.6 billion**, showing the impairment was non-cash. Cash was **$24.2 billion** and long-term debt **$16.0 billion** at June 30, 2026, on roughly **494 million** shares. Moat is **unproven**: scale and state contract relationships help, but medical-cost trend and redetermination risk cap reinvestment quality. Dhando is **pending** because downside from medical-cost spikes is not fully bounded. Proposed stance: **watch / pass** (base return below our **~15%** hurdle).

## Risks & inversion

**Primary risk:** Medical-cost trend running above premium yield, combined with Medicaid redetermination membership mix, can raise the health benefit ratio and compress earnings faster than Marketplace growth offsets.

- **Goodwill and intangibles:** **$6.7 billion** Magellan goodwill impairment in FY2025; further intangible charges would hit reported equity.
- **Regulatory rate resets:** Medicaid rate negotiations and Medicare Advantage benchmark changes can lag claim inflation.
- **Statutory capital:** Insurance subsidiaries must hold surplus; large cash at the parent may not be fully distributable.

**Inversion (what would make us wrong to pass):** Two consecutive quarters with consolidated health benefit ratio at or below guidance and net income growing **8%+** year over year would force reinvestment-rate and stance reassessment.

**Lens failure mode:** Treating consolidated operating cash flow as fully distributable ignores regulatory capital in insurance subsidiaries and periodic premium receivable swings that moved FY2024 cash flow to **$154 million**.

#### IRR arithmetic (show your work)

How we calculated the annual return (mechanical sync from `valuation_contract.json` calculation proof):

1. Growth from reinvestment = reinvestment rate × incremental return on capital (base: 20% × 10% = **2.0%** per year).
2. Year-one owner earnings = starting owner earnings × (1 + growth) = **$4,407 million**.
3. Distributable cash each year = owner earnings × (1 − reinvestment rate) = 80% of owner earnings.
4. Present value of seven years of distributable cash at the **12%** discount rate = **$16,938 million**.
5. Terminal value = year-seven owner earnings × **11×** terminal multiple = **$54,598 million**; discounted to present = **$24,697 million**.
6. Enterprise value = **$41,636 million** (sum of present values).
7. Equity value = enterprise value + cash − long-term debt = **$49,757 million**.
8. Value per share = equity value ÷ shares = **$100.72** (base component value).
9. Annual return at today's price: (**$100.72** ÷ **$64.34**)^(1/7) − 1 = **6.6% per year** (contract legacy audit: **6.61%**).

**Upside / downside from price:** Base annual return **6.6% per year** at **$64.34**; low case **3.2% per year** (intrinsic **$80.07/sh**); high case **10.8% per year** (intrinsic **$131.76/sh**). Upside to base intrinsic value is **~57%** from today's price.
