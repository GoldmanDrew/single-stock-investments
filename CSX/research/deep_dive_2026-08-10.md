## What this business is

CSX Corporation (NYSE: CSX) operates a Class I freight railroad across 23 eastern states, the District of Columbia, and two Canadian provinces. **[Fact]** FY2025 revenue was **$14.09 billion**, down about **3%** from the prior year per the 2026 proxy summary. **[Fact]** FY2025 operating cash flow was **$4.61 billion** and capital spending was **$2.90 billion**, leaving **$1.71 billion** of normalized owner earnings. **[Inference]** CSX is an eastern U.S. rail compounder: a high fixed-cost network with pricing power on captive lanes, cyclical exposure to industrial and intermodal volumes, and ongoing capital needs to maintain track and equipment.

---

## Why the market might be wrong

The market may be paying for **network durability and precision-scheduled railroading efficiency** while underweighting **$18.9 billion of total debt** on the June 2026 balance sheet. A stale 2012 debt tag had implied roughly half that leverage. **[Inference]** At **$50.11**, the stock trades near **7 times** our base proof value per share after refreshing debt from filings, so the market embeds reinvestment returns and terminal economics well above our bounded base case. Insider activity over the past year shows net selling exceeding open-market buys, which weakens a pure "insiders know more" bull case unless sales are largely planned programs.

## Risks & inversion

**Primary risk:** Cyclical freight downturn plus sustained **$2.9 billion** annual capital spending could stall owner-earnings growth while **$18.9 billion** of debt limits financial flexibility.

**Additional risks:**

- **Truck competition:** Intermodal yields face pressure when trucking capacity loosens.
- **Coal decline:** Legacy coal carloads continue to shrink as a revenue mix headwind.
- **Labor and service:** Crew availability and service metrics affect customer retention and regulatory scrutiny.
- **Activist overhang:** Historical Mantle Ridge campaign files remain in inventory; intent requires human review.

**Lens failure mode (operating):** If normalized owner earnings fall below **$1.2 billion** for two consecutive years while debt remains above **$17 billion**, the compounder narrative fails and the stock de-rates toward cyclical infrastructure multiples.

#### IRR arithmetic (show your work)

1. Growth from reinvestment = reinvestment rate × incremental return on capital = 0.35 × 0.18 = **6.3%** per year on owner earnings.
2. Year-one owner earnings = $1.71B × 1.063 = **$1.82B**; distributable cash at 65% payout of earnings = **$1.18B**.
3. Repeat growth on owner earnings for years 2–7; discount distributable cash flows at **10%**.
4. Terminal value = year-seven owner earnings × **18×**; discount to present at year 7.
5. Enterprise value = present value of distributable cash plus terminal present value.
6. Equity value = enterprise value + cash − debt = enterprise value + **$1.01B** − **$18.86B**.
7. Value per share = equity value ÷ **1.85B** shares ≈ **$7.12**.
8. Annual return at today's price = (value per share ÷ price today)^(1/7) − 1 = (**$7.12** ÷ **$50.11**)^(1/7) − 1 ≈ **-24.3%** per year.
