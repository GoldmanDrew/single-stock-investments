## What this business is
Dollar General Corporation (NYSE: DG) operates more than **20,000** small-box discount stores in **47** U.S. states, focused on rural and small-town communities where larger retailers are scarce. **[Fact]** FY2025 net sales were **$42.7 billion**, up from **$40.6 billion** in FY2024. **[Fact]** The mix is heavily consumables (food, health and beauty, cleaning), with seasonal, apparel, and home categories filling out the basket. **[Fact]** Stores average roughly **7,400 square feet**, use a low-cost build model, and rely on a centralized distribution network. **[Inference]** Dollar General is a high-turn consumables compounder whose economics depend on store density, purchasing scale, and reliable in-stock execution in price-sensitive markets.

---

## Why the market might be wrong
The market may be pricing a **recovery in same-store sales and margin** after shrink and supply-chain problems while underweighting **$4.6 billion of debt** still on the balance sheet and **$1.2 billion of annual capital spending** that already sits between operating cash flow and owner cash. **[Inference]** At **$126.59**, the stock trades below our refreshed proof base value per share, implying the market discounts reinvestment returns or terminal economics versus our bounded base case. **[Fact]** Insider activity over the past year shows modest open-market buying alongside larger planned sales, which weakens a pure "insiders know more" bull case unless those sales are largely 10b5-1 programs.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Persistent inventory shrink, supply-chain disruption, or aggressive price investment by Walmart and Dollar Tree could stall margin recovery while **$4.6 billion** of debt limits balance-sheet flexibility.

**Additional risks:**

- **Labor and turnover:** Store staffing challenges raise wage pressure and hurt customer experience.
- **Regulatory and litigation:** OSHA citations and wage-hour claims elevate compliance costs (10-K risk factors).
- **Store growth returns:** New-store productivity may fall if trade areas overlap or rural markets saturate.
- **Insider selling:** Net sales over the past year weaken insider-alignment signal despite selective open-market buys.

**Lens failure mode (operating):** If normalized owner earnings fall below **$2.0 billion** for two consecutive years while debt stays above **$4.5 billion**, the compounder narrative fails and the stock de-rates toward mid-single-digit retail multiples.
