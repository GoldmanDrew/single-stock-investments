## What this business is
Dino Polska runs a nationwide chain of mid-sized grocery stores in Poland, mostly in smaller towns and on the edges of larger cities. Shoppers buy everyday food with a heavy fresh mix (meat, produce, bakery) from a standardized format of roughly five thousand products. The company owns much of its logistics: distribution centers replenish stores on a tight cycle so shelves stay stocked without depending on wholesalers. At 31 March 2026 the network had 3,094 stores and twelve distribution centers. Almost all revenue is retail grocery sales in Poland.

---

## Why the market might be wrong
The share price fell hard from the 52-week high near PLN 52 toward the high twenties, even while sales kept growing mid-teens and the store count kept rising. Index and narrative money may treat Dino as a crowded Polish retail compounder whose accounting free cash flow looks thin because growth capital spending for new stores and centers consumes cash. The predictive attribute is reinvestment quality: cash from operations still covers growth CapEx, like-for-like sales stayed positive at 4.4%, and white space in several provinces is not exhausted. The inefficiency is confusing growth CapEx with a broken cash engine.

---

## Executive summary
Dino is a scale grocery compounder in Poland with owned logistics and a proximity store format aimed at towns larger chains underserve. Full-year 2025 sales were PLN 33.6 billion (+14.9%), EBITDA PLN 2.55 billion (+9.9%), and net profit PLN 1.56 billion. Q1 2026 sales rose 14.8% with another 62 net new stores. At about PLN 29.45 per share, using owner earnings of roughly PLN 2.24 per share (operating cash flow minus depreciation as a maintenance proxy), the base case annual return is about 21% per year over 7 years. Accounting free cash flow after growth build is only about PLN 0.62 per share and would imply a poor return if mistaken for steady-state owner cash. Competitive pressure from discounters and wage costs are real; the bear case still shows about 10% per year, not permanent capital loss. Primary stance proposal from the model is accumulate if the human accepts the owner-earnings starting point; actionable capital stance remains a human decision.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Discounters and wage inflation compress like-for-like sales and EBITDA margin while the company is still spending heavily on openings, so owner earnings growth stalls and the multiple falls together.

- Single-country Poland exposure (FX, regulation, consumer).
- CapEx intensity: if openings miss returns, accounting FCF stays depressed longer than modeled.
- Governance / liquidity: WSE listing, concentrated ownership, limited English research coverage.

**Lens failure mode (operating):** If growth CapEx is actually maintenance in disguise (replacement of a depreciating network without real white space), starting owner earnings of PLN 2.24 is too high and the base return collapses toward the accounting-FCF path.
