## What this business is
DaVita Inc. (NYSE: DVA) operates kidney dialysis centers and related lab services for patients with chronic kidney failure, primarily in the United States. **[Fact]** FY2025 revenue was **$13.64 billion** and operating income was **$2.04 billion**. **[Fact]** FY2025 operating cash flow was **$1.89 billion** and capital spending was **$0.58 billion**, leaving **$1.31 billion** of normalized owner earnings. **[Fact]** The company reports a **single operating segment** (U.S. dialysis and related lab services). **[Inference]** DaVita is a scale-driven healthcare operator whose economics depend on treatment volumes, payer mix (Medicare, Medicare Advantage, commercial), and disciplined center-level cost control.

---

## Why the market might be wrong
The market may be pricing **durable dialysis demand and operating leverage** while underweighting **$10.5 billion of long-term debt and capital lease obligations** on the March 2026 balance sheet and **thin GAAP equity** (**$651 million** of stockholders equity at year-end 2025). **[Inference]** At **$234.10**, the stock trades near our base proof value per share after refreshing debt from filings, implying the market expects reinvestment returns and terminal economics in line with our bounded base case rather than a de-leveraging discount. Berkshire Hathaway has held a large activist stake (SC-13D/A filings, context tier only); that signal supports quality-of-business but does not, by itself, justify rich price or net debt.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Medicare and Medicare Advantage reimbursement changes plus **$10.5 billion** of debt could compress owner cash faster than treatment volumes grow.

**Additional risks:**

- **Payer mix:** Commercial rate pressure or MA coding changes can move margins quickly.
- **Labor and center costs:** Nurse and technician wages affect center-level profitability.
- **Leverage and refinancing:** Higher rates or tighter covenants limit flexibility if owner cash weakens.
- **Policy / regulatory:** CMS rate-setting and litigation over payment models remain ongoing overhangs.

**Lens failure mode (operating):** If normalized owner earnings fall below **$1.0 billion** for two consecutive years while debt remains above **$10 billion**, the compounder narrative fails and the stock de-rates toward leveraged healthcare operator multiples.
