## What this business is
Innventure identifies technologies inside large corporations, then creates and operates new companies to commercialize them. The public ticker consolidates several operating companies, led today by Accelsius (two-phase liquid cooling for AI and high-performance computing), with AeroFlexx (flexible packaging) and Refinity (plastics/olefins) earlier in the life cycle. PureCycle was an earlier Innventure company that is now a separate public equity. At the parent, reported revenue is still very small and losses are large while the subsidiaries raise capital and try to scale.

---

## Why the market might be wrong
The market may be pricing INV as a thin, cash-burning holding company rather than as a claim on Accelsius's cooling franchise if two-phase cooling becomes required as chip power rises. The predictive attribute is commercial adoption of Accelsius systems at hyperscalers and high-density sites, not near-term consolidated earnings. Inefficiency comes from complexity: multi-entity capital structure, preferreds and noncontrolling interests, and a short public history after the Learn CW business combination.

---

Innventure is a company-creation platform. Accelsius is the economic center of gravity. ## Business & moat
### What (Stahl + Lawrence)

Stahl archetype: **holding_co** / company-creation platform. Lawrence bucket: **other** (special-situation asset claim, not classic pricing-power compounder).

Innventure's model is to source protected technology from multinationals, stand up operating companies, fund them, and either scale them or exit. Public shareholders own the consolidating parent, not a clean single-product P&L.

### Mental models

- **Platform vs product:** Parent valuation depends on subsidiary economics and ownership after preferred and strategic rounds.
- **Adoption / innovator's dilemma:** Two-phase cooling may be "better now, required later," but conservative data-center buyers move slowly.
- **Incentive-caused bias:** Operating-company management may prefer a private exit path over optimizing the public parent.
- **Inversion:** Ask what fails first: chip adoption timing, competitor catch-up (Vertiv and others), capital markets closing, or dilution of INV's Accelsius claim.

### Business mechanics (Hohn)

**Operating snapshot (Q1 2026, 10-Q):** Revenue about $1.4 million. Unrestricted cash about $55.4 million plus $5.0 million restricted. Net loss attributable to stockholders about $20.8 million. Weighted-average common shares about 77.8 million. The balance sheet still carries large intangible assets from the business combination and shows material equity attributable to noncontrolling interests.

**Thesis pillars**

1. Accelsius sits in the path of rising GPU power density where single-phase cooling may become insufficient.
2. Strategic investors (Johnson Controls, Legrand per Magis notes) can open channels Accelsius cannot build alone. Context only until approved.
3. Innventure has a repeatable company-creation playbook (PureCycle precedent), but public INV today is mostly Accelsius optionality plus cash burn.

### Moat (Munger)

Moat at the parent is **unproven**. Accelsius may have a technical lead in two-phase direct-to-chip systems, but moat durability depends on patents, reference designs (for example AMD), manufacturing scale, and whether OEMs (Vertiv and peers) close the gap. AeroFlexx and Refinity are not yet moat evidence.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Accelsius adoption stalls while the parent burns cash and dilutes.

- Capital markets risk: shelf registrations and preferred structures can reprice ownership of the upside.
- Technology / competitive risk: OEMs with distribution may catch up even if Accelsius is ahead today.
- Governance risk: incentives between Accelsius management, strategics, and INV public holders may diverge.
- Lens failure mode (asset): if Accelsius never becomes a transferable franchise, the "asset claim" collapses to a cash-burn stub.
