# NU competition hypothesis — Mercado Pago (MELI) and Sea Monee

**Date:** 2026-07-28  
**Agent:** Marvin  
**Question:** Will NU face *increasingly difficult* competition from Mercado Libre’s Mercado Pago and Sea’s Monee because those platforms have useful proprietary Brazil customer data?

**Verdict (plain English):** **Partially yes on Mercado Pago; early and narrower on Sea Monee.** Proprietary commerce data is a real underwriting edge in *merchant and checkout-linked credit*. It does **not** automatically displace Nu’s primary-bank franchise (deposits, Pix, cards as daily money home). Expect **tougher credit-card and BNPL share battles** and some pressure on risk-adjusted margins, not a wholesale erosion of Nu’s deposit machine over the next few years.

---

## Hypothesis restated

1. MELI and Sea sit on purchase / seller behavior that Nu cannot see as cleanly.  
2. That data improves credit models → cheaper acquisition + better risk selection.  
3. Therefore Nu’s growth and returns get harder year by year.

## What primary filings say

### Nu’s own competitive map (FY2025 20-F)

Named Brazilian payments competitors include **MercadoPago Instituição de Pagamento Ltda.**, PicPay, PagSeguro, StoneCo. Named credit/investments peers include Itaú, Bradesco, Santander Brasil, Inter, C6, XP.  

**Sea / Shopee / Monee do not appear** in the FY2023–FY2025 20-Fs. That is not proof they are irrelevant; it is proof they are still **below Nu’s disclosed material-competitor bar** (or framed as general “fintech / non-financial company” competition).

Nu also flags **Open Finance**: sharing standardized customer data can make it easier for rivals to offer better credit terms and pull obligations off Nu’s platform.

**Source:** `NU/investor-documents/sec-edgar/20-F_20260408_rpt20251231_acc0001292814_26_002166.htm` (Item 3 competition / credit model risks; Item 4 underwriting).

### Nu’s own data moat (same filing)

- NuX credit engine; management cites **60+** machine-learning algorithms.  
- Mexico case: sixth-generation models claimed **11 percentage points** lower risk at comparable approval vs generic bureau scores.  
- **>62%** of active customers treat Nu as primary bank (≥50% of post-tax income flowing through NuAccount).  
- Deposits **US$41.9B**, **92.5%** demand; Brazil funding cost **81% of interbank** (Dec 2025).  
- Monthly cost to serve **US$0.8** vs ARPAC **US$13.3**.

Commerce data is powerful for *purchase-linked* credit. Nu’s proprietary stack is *relationship + cash-flow + payment behavior* across a full money platform, including deposits Nu funds itself.

## Scorecard — does proprietary data make competition “increasingly difficult”?

| Arena | MELI Mercado Pago | Sea Monee | Nu position |
|-------|-------------------|-----------|-------------|
| Checkout / BNPL / seller credit | **Strong edge** (marketplace GMV + seller P&L) | **Strong edge inside Shopee**; Brazil early | Competes, but not Nu’s core identity |
| Consumer credit cards | **Rising fast**; sharing growth with Nu | Secondary for now | Still largest digital volume / profit in card growth narrative |
| Demand deposits / primary account | Weaker historically (wallet-first) | SeaBank-style elsewhere; Brazil credit-led so far | **Nu’s fortress** (US$41.9B deposits, primary-bank share) |
| Cost to acquire credit customers | Low via MELI traffic | Low via Shopee | Higher organic digital CAC but huge brand + viral base |
| Full-stack money platform (invest, insure, Pix, multi-country bank) | Building | Building in Brazil | **Years ahead** in Brazil customers (113M) |

**Inference:** Data advantage is **product-specific**, not **company-fatal**. The hypothesis is strongest for *credit origination share and risk selection at the margin*. It is weakest for *deposit franchise displacement*.

## Mechanisms that bound the bear case

1. **Different data, different products.** Commerce graphs predict buy-now behavior; bank ledgers predict income, bill pay, and deposit stickiness. Open Finance narrows both edges over time.  
2. **Scale and primary-bank status.** Once salary and bills sit in NuAccount, switching costs rise even if a rival offers a better card APR.  
3. **Funding.** Nu already funds at a discount to CDI on a large organic deposit base. Marketplace lenders often fund with prepaid balances, wholesale, or parent capital; that can look cheap early and reprice later.  
4. **Monee size.** At ~US$1B Brazil loans vs Nu’s tens of billions of portfolio, Monee is a **high-growth niche**, not yet a system-level peer in Nu’s disclosed competitive set.  
5. **Credit cycle is the shared risk.** If Brazil consumer credit turns, *all three* feel it; proprietary data helps ranking borrowers, not eliminating cyclical loss.

## What would falsify this memo

- Nu Brazil **primary-bank share** or activity rate rolls over for several quarters while Mercado Pago credit customers and card stock keep compounding faster than Nu.  
- Nu **cost of funding** drifts toward 100% of CDI *and* NPL rises without ARPAC offset.  
- Sea Monee Brazil loan book approaches **mid–tens of billions** with stable NPLs *and* launches a competitive deposit franchise.  
- Open Finance plus MELI credit terms visibly **pull revolving balances** off Nu at scale (filed evidence, not anecdote).

## Portfolio implication for NU stance

Bake **competition into growth, not into a binary “moat broken” call**. Base valuation already normalizes owner-cash growth to **17% then 9%** (well below FY2025 NI +45%) partly for this reason. See `NU/research/valuation.json` and `deep_dive_2026-07-28.md`.

**Bottom line:** Yes, Mercado Pago (and later Monee) make *credit* competition harder using proprietary Brazil customer data. No, that alone does not imply Nu loses the *banking* war. MS AlphaWise is the cleanest third-party support for that split (usage vs primary). Watch deposit share, primary-bank %, risk-adjusted NIM, and card stock growth splits vs MELI each quarter.
