## What this business is
Nu Holdings Ltd. (ticker **NU**) is a Cayman holding company for a digital money platform operating mainly in **Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia**. **[Fact]** As of December 31, 2025 it served **131 million** customers with credit and prepaid cards, demand deposits and Money Boxes, Pix payments, personal and SME loans, investments, and insurance distribution. **[Fact]** FY2025 total revenue was **US$15.8 billion** (+37%) and net income attributable to the parent was **US$2.87 billion** (+45%). **[Fact]** Customers fund the balance sheet with **US$41.9 billion** of deposits, mostly payable on demand, while an interest-earning credit book and card receivables drive net interest and fee income. Nu presents results in U.S. dollars under IFRS and files a Form 20-F.

---

## Why the market might be wrong
**[Inference]** At about **US$14.68** per share, the market may treat Nu as a generic high-growth fintech priced for perfect credit outcomes, or conversely as a bank forever capped by Brazil rates, without separating the **deposit franchise** (hard to copy) from **credit origination** (increasingly contested by Mercado Pago and, later, Sea Monee). **[Inference]** Unit economics in the FY2025 20-F (ARPAC **US$13.3**, cost to serve **US$0.8**, Brazil funding near **81% of CDI**) support a durable compounding engine even if credit growth cools from last year’s pace. **[Inference]** US national-bank optionality (OCC preliminary conditional approval January 2026) is easy to overpay for and easy to ignore; we leave it mostly outside base owner cash.

---

## Executive summary
Nu is Latin America’s scaled digital bank: **131 million** customers, **US$41.9 billion** deposits, and **US$2.87 billion** of FY2025 owner earnings power. Competition in **cards and BNPL** from Mercado Pago is real and intensifying; Sea’s Monee is a fast but still small Brazil credit book. We normalize growth well below last year’s **45%** profit jump. At **US$14.68**, our base case implies about **16.06%** per year over 7 years. Dhando is **partial**. ## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Marketplace lenders (especially Mercado Pago) use commerce data to take credit-card and BNPL share while Open Finance makes balances easier to move, compressing Nu’s risk-adjusted growth.

1. **Credit competition / data:** Mercado Pago (and later Monee) use commerce graphs to originate cards and BNPL cheaper; Open Finance helps rivals peel credit.  
2. **Credit cycle:** NPL 6.6% already elevated vs pristine books; low-and-grow strategy can look wrong in a downturn.  
3. **Funding / rates:** Deposit costs rose with volume; path back toward 100% of CDI hurts spread.  
4. **Regulation / capital:** Model governance, Open Finance, and any US bank build-out raise capital and compliance spend.  
5. **FX / Brazil macro:** USD reporting masks BRL volatility for local economics.  
6. **Governance concentration:** High Class B voting power.

**Inversion:** If Nu is *not* a good investment at this price, it is because credit losses and marketplace share gains cut owner-cash growth into high single digits while the multiple compresses, not because the app stops working.
