## Executive summary

These two documents are not arguing about the same security on the same clock.

McIntyre (3 Aug, **before** the print) is pricing a **2029 RemainCo** after a **~$1.5B point-of-care sale**: ~2.5× levered legacy Ortho, 12× 2029 EBITDA, **~$100/share**. Jefferies (6 Aug, **after** the print) is pricing **consolidated 2026–27 cash as it stands**: no sale, free cash flow withdrawn, China 2H down more than 25%, **Hold / $12** from a 10-year DCF at ~6× 2027 EBITDA.

The Q2 print **confirms both half-stories**. Core Labs and immunohematology **outside China** grew 9% and 5%. China fell 23% constant currency. Adjusted EBITDA beat. Then management **cut** full-year revenue by ~$165M, EBITDA by ~$75M, and **withdrew** the $100–120M free-cash-flow guide. Q2 free cash flow was **−$136M**. Net leverage **4.3×**. On the call, the CEO **refused to confirm** the Financial Times sale rumor.

**House synthesis:** Jefferies is the better map of **2026 owner cash and leverage risk**. McIntyre is still the better map of **what the core franchise is worth if the sale closes**. Our May blend (hold, partial dhando, not McIntyre-scale concentration) survives. The Q1-based Lawrence path of **$1.65/share** owner cash from a **$622.5M** EBITDA midpoint is **falsified for 2026**. Do not adopt $100 or $12 as the number. Stance stays **hold** until one of three clocks rings: consecutive positive free cash flow, China NHSA finalization with a sized price cut, or an 8-K on a POC sale.

Jefferies is **[PENDING APPROVAL]**. McIntyre remains the only approved third party; the Q2 letter **raises** the external target from ~$60 (2028) to ~$100 (2029) without new filing proof.

## Divergences (the actual argument)

### 1. Clock and cash conversion

McIntyre, 3 Aug: *"I believe Q2 will mark a low point for cash flow generation."* That was a **forecast**. Q2 FCF of −$136M is consistent with a trough **print**. It does not prove the trough **is behind us**. H1 operating cash is already −$144M. The $25M Grifols payment and $97M Lex outlay are partly one-off, but working capital and China destock are not.

Jefferies: after several FCF misses, withdrawing the guide is strike three. They "struggle to find any valuation that makes sense when FCF continues to deteriorate" at 4.3×. Their base case is **positive FCF in 2027**, not 2026.

**House:** McIntyre's trough claim is now the **central 2026 test**, not a given. Q3 and Q4 FCF (ex Grifols/Lex) decide it. Until then, do not underwrite 2026 owner cash of $1.65/share. Even 18% of the **new** $550M EBITDA midpoint is only ~$1.45/share, and 18% conversion is not what H1 cash did.

### 2. China duration

McIntyre: Chinese regulations "finalized in the coming months," then legacy Ortho returns to historical **high-single-digit EBITDA growth**.

Jefferies (management callback): path into **2027 is unclear**. 2H China revenue down more than 25% vs 1H run-rate. Destocking often gets worse before VBP/DRG clears.

**House:** Q2 is Jefferies' inning. "Coming months" is possible but not evidenced. Size China as a **2026–27 earnings hole**, not a Q3 switch-flip. Do not kill the core franchise because China destocked.

### 3. Does POC get sold, and is POC even the bad asset?

McIntyre treats POC as the old Quidel problem child (flu/COVID), sale undoes the 2022 merger, RemainCo is clean Ortho.

Q2 POC **grew 16%**, led by Triage (+10%) and other cardiac (>40% in Jefferies). Respiratory is the soft piece; cardiac POC is not dying. Selling "POC" at $1.5B might be selling a **mix of declining respiratory and improving cardiac**, not dumping a corpse.

Jefferies: no deal in the model. Upside case ($30) is "headwinds clear," not "sale closes."

**House:** keep the sale as **milestone_nav**, zero in base, until 8-K. Do not assume $1.5B is a floor. Respiratory-weak POC can clear **below** 10× if buyers underwrite flu risk. Cardiac strength cuts the other way.

### 4. Multiple: 6× vs 12×

Spot EV at ~$12.55 and ~$2.77B net debt on ~$550M guided EBITDA is still ~**6×**. That is Jefferies' 2027 multiple, not a deep-value accident.

McIntyre 12× on 2029 RemainCo EBITDA of roughly **$675M** is what you need for ~$100 (see arithmetic below). That is **peer-compounder** multiple plus **three years of HSD growth after a recap**. Jefferies' own upside is 8× → $30, which is the "it works but the Street does not pay 12×" world.

**House:** 9–10× on **demonstrated** owner cash remains the Lawrence exit. 12–15× is a **bull overlay** after de-lever and China clarity, not the base. 6× is what the market pays for a 4.3× levered name with no FCF guide.

### 5. McIntyre raised the target into worse cash

Q1 letter (approved 28 May): ~$4 FCF/share by 2028, 15× → **~$60**.
Q2 letter: 12× 2029 RemainCo EBITDA → **~$100**.

The external bull case **got more aggressive** in the quarter the company **cut EBITDA 12%** and pulled FCF. That is incentive-plus-commitment bias (concentrated, closed fund, QDEL already cost >500 bps). It does not make the RemainCo math illegal. It does mean we should **not** increase McIntyre's weight in the blend.

## Arithmetic (show the work)

Shares ~**68.3M** (Q2 basic). Net debt ~**$2,768M** (gross $2,891 − cash $123).

**Jefferies base (no sale):** 6× $576M 2027 EBITDA = $3,456M EV − $2,768M = $688M equity ≈ **$10/share**, in the neighborhood of the $12 DCF. 8× same EBITDA ≈ **$27/share** (their $30 upside). 5× ≈ **$1–2/share** accounting equity; their $5 downside is the DCF floor, not a liquidation.

**McIntyre RemainCo at $1.5B proceeds:** net debt → ~$1,268M. FY25-style core EBITDA ~$480M (Labs+IH allocation in `qdel_data.json`) → leverage ~**2.6×**. 12× $480M = $5,760M EV − $1,268M = $4,492M ≈ **$66/share**. To hit **$100**, 2029 EBITDA needs to be about **$675M** ($100 × 68.3M + $1,268M, ÷ 12). That is ~12% annual EBITDA growth from $480M over three years, or HSD growth from a higher 2026 core base. **Possible if China normalizes and cost-out sticks. Not the 2026 guide.**

**Stale Marvin $1.65/share:** 0.18 × $622.5M / 67.8M. New midpoint $550M at the same 18% haircut is **$1.45/share** — and 18% is optimistic versus H1 cash. Proof-first contract base **$8.68/share** at $13.79 implied **−6.4%** annualized. At $12.55 that gap narrows but the **cash assumption still has to be rebuilt** after the 10-Q.

**SOTP ~$49 / post-sale ~$83** in `valuation_assumption_ledger.md` used **old** $615–630M EBITDA and FY25 segment mix. Those equity figures are **upward-biased** until rebuilt.
