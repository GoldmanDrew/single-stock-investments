## What this business is
Transocean Ltd. is a Swiss-domiciled offshore contract driller operating a fleet of **27 mobile offshore drilling units** focused on ultra-deepwater and harsh-environment floaters. **[Fact]** FY2025 contract revenue was **$3.97 billion**, up from **$3.52 billion** in 2024, per `RIG/investor-documents/sec-edgar/10-K_20260223_rpt20251231_acc0001451505_26_000018.htm`. **[Fact]** The company reported FY2025 operating cash flow of **$749 million**, sustaining capital spending of **$123 million**, and a net loss of **$2.92 billion** driven by rig impairments and depreciation rather than a collapse in customer collections. **[Fact]** At March 31, 2026 the balance sheet showed **$615 million** of cash, **$4.95 billion** of long-term debt and capital lease obligations, and **$8.19 billion** of stockholders' equity on **1.12 billion** shares outstanding per `RIG/investor-documents/sec-edgar/10-Q_20260505_rpt20260331_acc0001451505_26_000044.htm`.

---

## Why the market might be wrong
The stock may be treating headline revenue growth and Q1 2026 profitability as proof that equity is cheap on normalized cash, while our component base still embeds **heavy net debt per share** and **zero Valaris synergy credit**. **[Inference]** Generalists may capitalize FY2025 operating cash flow without adjusting for mid-cycle utilization, cash interest, or the September 2025 **125 million** share equity offering. **[Inference]** Conversely, bears may treat the FY2025 net loss as recurring and ignore **72.4%** fleet utilization, **96.5%** revenue efficiency, and **$6.06 billion** of contract backlog at February 19, 2026. **[Inference]** Customer concentration with Equinor and Shell near **22%** of revenue each means backlog quality is tied to a handful of deepwater budgets that can shift quickly.

---

## Executive summary
Transocean is the scale leader in ultra-deepwater drilling, exiting a trough with better utilization and a large backlog, but equity sits behind roughly **$4.3 billion** of net debt and more than **1.1 billion** shares. At about **$5.86** per share, we expect about **3.6% per year** over seven years on mid-cycle normalized owner cash of **$0.36** per share. Component proofs sum to roughly **negative to low single-digit dollars** per share before any merger synergy, so the market is paying for cycle recovery and the Valaris option. We propose **watch**.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Offshore spending slows while **$4.9B+** of debt matures or refinances, forcing dilutive equity or distressed exchanges.

**Secondary risks:**

- Customer concentration: Equinor / Shell **22%** revenue each
- Contract cancellations or force majeure during downturns
- Valaris merger fails or closes with worse leverage than modeled
- Continued rig impairments if stacked units lack future work

**Lens failure mode (operating / cyclical):** Peak dayrates and utilization get capitalized into equity value while supply response (newbuilds, merged fleets, onshore alternatives) caps mid-cycle owner cash.

**Inversion:** What would make this a good investment? Sustained **80%+** utilization, backlog growth (not shrink), net debt below **$3B** without massive dilution, and Valaris synergies that drop cash interest per rig.
