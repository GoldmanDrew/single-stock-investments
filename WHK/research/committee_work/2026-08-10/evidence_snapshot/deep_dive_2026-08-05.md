## What this business is
WhiteHawk Minerals Corp. (NYSE: WHK) is a natural gas mineral and royalty company. **[Fact]** It owns non-operated mineral and royalty interests concentrated in the Marcellus Shale (Appalachian Basin) and the Haynesville Shale (Haynesville Basin), two of the lowest-cost U.S. natural gas basins, and it collects a royalty share of wellhead revenue without operating any wells itself. **[Fact, 424B4 prospectus and investor-relations site]** The company grew mainly by acquisition, closing the PHX Minerals acquisition on June 23, 2025 and the Three Rivers Royalty ("TRR") acquisition, which is why pro forma full-year 2025 revenue ($89.2 million) is roughly nine times larger than actual 2024 revenue ($9.5 million). **[Fact]** WhiteHawk completed its initial public offering on June 10, 2026, raising about $200.2 million in gross proceeds from the sale of 7,700,000 Class A shares at $26.00 per share, through an operating partnership ("Up-C") structure, and simultaneously internalized its management function by buying out its external manager for $130.0 million. **[Fact]**

## Why the market might be wrong
WhiteHawk is a newly public, thinly followed natural gas mineral and royalty aggregator with no analyst estimates or dividend history yet, so any mispricing today is more likely to come from a lack of coverage than from a mispriced narrative. **[Opinion]** The 424B4 prospectus discloses a company-defined non-GAAP measure, Cash Available for Distribution, that reached $36.3 million on a pro forma full-year 2025 basis; against a roughly $600 million pro forma equity value at the $26.00 IPO price, that implies a mid-single-digit percent cash yield before any dividend has actually been declared. **[Fact]** Whether the market is right to wait for the first declared dividend, or is over-discounting a business that already has audited, filed cash flow, is the open question; we do not yet have enough evidence to call it either way. **[Opinion]** The company also restated its FY2025 financial statements on April 22, 2026, seven weeks before the IPO, for material accounting errors, and identified material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting; that is a real reason a cautious market could discount the stock beyond what the underlying royalty cash flow alone would suggest. **[Fact, 424B4 Risk Factors]**

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Anchoring on the pro forma Cash Available for Distribution figure ($36.3 million FY2025) as if it were an already-declared dividend, when no payout policy exists yet, and underweighting the FY2025 restatement and internal-control material weaknesses because they are described in dense prospectus language rather than a standalone accounting-fraud headline.

- **Fresh accounting-quality risk:** On April 22, 2026, seven weeks before the IPO, WhiteHawk concluded its FY2025 audited financial statements could no longer be relied upon due to material accounting errors (management-fee recording; misclassification of acquisition-related cash) and identified material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting (`investor-documents/sec-edgar/424B4_20260609_rpt_acc0001193125_26_264014.htm`, Risk Factors).
- **Earnout incentive risk:** the former external manager's principals can earn up to 1,250,000 additional Class B shares and OpCo units by hitting Adjusted EBITDA targets that the company itself defines and reports over the next three years (8-K Item 2.01).
- **Undeclared dividend policy risk:** the entire premise of a mineral/royalty income vehicle is cash returned to shareholders, and WhiteHawk has declared none as a public company; Cash Available for Distribution is a company-defined non-GAAP measure, not a committed payout.
- **Commodity-price and reserve-decline risk:** royalty revenue is a direct, unhedged pass-through of natural gas prices and operator-controlled production volumes on wells WhiteHawk does not operate; a sustained low-gas-price environment or faster-than-expected decline in the underlying Marcellus/Haynesville wells would reduce Cash Available for Distribution with no operating levers (drilling, cost cuts) available to management to offset it.
- **Concentration risk:** the entire post-Internalization asset base is two shale basins (Marcellus and Haynesville) built mainly from two acquisitions (PHX Minerals, Three Rivers Royalty) closed within the same twelve months; there is no evidence yet of a diversified, multi-decade acquisition track record.
