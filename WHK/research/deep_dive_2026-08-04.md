## What this business is
WhiteHawk Minerals Corp. (NYSE: WHK) is a Delaware corporation that completed its initial public offering on June 10, 2026, raising about $200.2 million in gross proceeds from the sale of 7,700,000 Class A shares at $26.00 per share. **[Fact]** The company owns its business through an operating partnership, WhiteHawk Income Operating Partnership L.P. ("WhiteHawk OpCo"), an "Up-C" structure common among newly listed real-asset and royalty companies. **[Fact]** At the IPO, WhiteHawk internalized its management function, buying out its external manager for $130.0 million and becoming self-managed. **[Fact]** The company's own name change from "WhiteHawk Income Corporation" to "WhiteHawk Minerals Corp." at the moment of listing, together with the "Income Operating Partnership" name, points toward a mineral or royalty income vehicle. **[Inference]** The only public filing we have (a closing 8-K) does not describe the underlying mineral or royalty assets, so we cannot yet state what WhiteHawk actually owns or how it earns money.

## Executive summary
WhiteHawk Minerals Corp. is a newly public, internally managed mineral or royalty income company organized as an "Up-C" around WhiteHawk Income Operating Partnership L.P. It raised about $200.2 million in gross proceeds at its June 10, 2026 initial public offering on the New York Stock Exchange and simultaneously bought out its external manager for $130.0 million, three quarters paid immediately in operating partnership units and Class B stock and one quarter tied to an earnout on future earnings. The company also redeemed roughly $39.9 million of preferred stock and amended its revolving credit facility. None of this tells us what WhiteHawk actually owns, how much cash it generates, or what its share count looks like on a fully diluted basis, because no income statement, balance sheet, or prospectus text is in the evidence record yet. We do not have enough evidence to compute an annual return at today's price, and we are not going to invent one. The record stays **pending** until the company files its first quarterly report or the Internalization pro forma financial statements promised in its closing 8-K. Stance: **watch**.

## Risks & inversion
**Primary risk:** Forming a view on WhiteHawk before its first public financial statements exist, and anchoring on the story (mineral or royalty income, newly internalized management, clean IPO balance sheet) rather than filed numbers.

- **Earnout incentive risk:** the former external manager's principals can earn up to 1,250,000 additional Class B shares and OpCo units by hitting Adjusted EBITDA targets that the company itself will define and report over the next three years (8-K Item 2.01).
- **Untested internal management:** the company became self-managed at the moment of listing, so there is no public track record yet of how the internalized team runs the business day to day.
- **Evidence gap:** the 424B4 prospectus that would describe the actual assets, revenue model, and any pre-IPO financial history is public but not yet in this ticker's evidence folder, and no 10-Q or 8-K/A financial statements had appeared in SEC EDGAR as of this review (2026-08-04).

**Lens failure mode:** If we are wrong that "wait for evidence" is the right call, and instead the market re-rates the stock sharply on early operating results before we act, the cost is a missed entry point, not a capital loss, since we have not committed capital or a stance beyond **watch**.
